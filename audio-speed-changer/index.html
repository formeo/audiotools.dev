Slow down or speed up audio without changing pitch

Audio Speed Changer is an online audio slow downer and speed changer. It plays any recording from 0.25x (four times slower) to 4x faster and keeps the original pitch, so a slowed-down song stays in its key and a sped-up voice does not turn into a chipmunk. It runs in your browser: nothing is uploaded.

How to slow down an audio file

  1. Drop an MP3, M4A, WAV or other audio file on the box above.
  2. Pick a preset (0.5x, 0.75x, 1.25x, 1.5x, 2x) or set any speed from 0.25x to 4x with the slider.
  3. Click “Apply & Process” and listen to the result.
  4. Download it as MP3 or WAV.

Slow down a song to learn it

Guitar solos, piano parts and drum fills are easier to learn at 0.5x–0.75x. Because the pitch stays the same, you can play along in the original key. To loop one passage, cut it out first with the audio trimmer, then slow it down here.

Slow down speech for language learning and transcription

At 0.75x, native speakers become much easier to follow without sounding unnatural. For typing up an interview or a lecture by hand, 0.5x–0.75x lets you keep up with the speaker.

Speed up podcasts, lectures and voice messages

At 1.5x, a 60-minute lecture takes 40 minutes; at 2x, 30. The result is a shorter file you can keep and play anywhere, not just a faster player setting.

Pitch stays put

A naive speed change also shifts pitch: higher when sped up, lower when slowed down. This tool uses WSOLA (Waveform Similarity Overlap-Add) time-stretching, which changes the tempo while keeping the original pitch, so voices and instruments sound natural.

Supported formats

MP3, M4A/AAC, WAV, FLAC, OGG and WebM: anything your browser can play. The result is saved as MP3 or WAV; for M4A or FLAC, convert it afterwards with the audio converter.

Want the words instead? Voice to text transcribes a recording on your device. To join several recordings first, use the audio joiner.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I slow down an audio file without changing the pitch?
Drop the file here, choose a speed below 1x (for example 0.75x) and click Apply & Process. WSOLA time-stretching keeps the original pitch; download the result as MP3 or WAV.
Can I slow down an M4A or WAV file?
Yes. MP3, M4A/AAC, WAV, FLAC, OGG and WebM all work. The result is saved as MP3 or WAV.
What speed is best for learning a song?
Start at 0.5x–0.75x and raise the speed as the part becomes comfortable. The pitch stays the same, so you can play along in the original key.
Can I speed up a podcast or voice message?
Yes. Choose 1.25x–2x and download the result. A 60-minute recording takes 40 minutes at 1.5x.
Is my audio uploaded?
No. Everything happens in your browser using the Web Audio API.
