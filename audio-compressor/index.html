    Compress MP3 and other audio files online

    Audio Compressor makes audio files smaller by re-encoding them to MP3 at the bitrate and sample rate you choose. It shows the expected size before you start, handles one file or a whole batch, and runs in your browser with the LAME MP3 encoder compiled to WebAssembly, so nothing is uploaded.

    How to compress an audio file

    1. Drop one or several files on the box above (MP3, WAV, M4A, FLAC, OGG).
    2. Choose a bitrate; for speech, a lower sample rate makes the file smaller still.
    3. Check the estimated size.
    4. Click “Compress & Download”. With several files, download each one or all of them as a ZIP.

    How small will the file be?

    An MP3's size depends only on its bitrate and length: about 1 MB per minute at 128 kbps and about 0.5 MB per minute at 64 kbps. A 10-minute recording is roughly 10 MB at 128 kbps and 5 MB at 64 kbps.

    Which bitrate to choose

    • 64 kbps — voice recordings, lectures and podcasts (smallest files)
    • 128 kbps — general purpose, good for most uses
    • 192 kbps — music in high quality
    • 320 kbps — maximum quality, for when size matters least

    Fit email and chat limits

    Most email services cap attachments at 20–25 MB: roughly 20–25 minutes of audio at 128 kbps, or 40–50 minutes at 64 kbps. For voice, 64 kbps at 22050 Hz keeps speech clear at the smallest size.

    WAV to MP3: the biggest saving

    Uncompressed WAV (CD quality) takes about 10.6 MB per minute. Compressing it to MP3 at 128 kbps makes it about 11 times smaller with little audible difference for most uses.

    Need other edits? Join files first with the audio joiner, cut silence at the ends with the audio trimmer, make a quiet recording louder with the volume booster, or save M4A or FLAC with the audio converter.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I reduce the size of an MP3 file?
    Re-encode it at a lower bitrate. Drop the MP3 here, pick 128 kbps or 64 kbps, and download the smaller file. Lowering the sample rate to 22050 Hz shrinks voice recordings further.
    How much smaller will my file be?
    About 1 MB per minute at 128 kbps and 0.5 MB per minute at 64 kbps. A WAV file usually becomes 5 to 20 times smaller. The tool shows the estimate before you compress.
    Does compression affect quality?
    Lossy compression removes some data. At 192 kbps or higher, most listeners cannot tell the difference from the original; for speech, 64 kbps is enough.
    Can I compress several files at once?
    Yes. Drop several files: all of them are compressed with the same settings, and you can download each one or all of them as a ZIP.
    Which sample rate should I use for voice?
    22050 Hz keeps speech clear and makes the file smaller. 16000 Hz is the smallest and still fine for speech, but not for music.
    Is my audio uploaded?
    No. All compression happens in your browser.
